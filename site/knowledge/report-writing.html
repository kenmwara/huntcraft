Report writing: how to turn a solved hunt into a report that scores

Solving the hunt is half the work. The report is what gets marked, and it is what a reader learns from. This is the method behind a good one, built on the Range's marking rubric and the lessons from a past hunt's debrief.

The reader and the bottom line

A reviewer reads the first paragraph and decides what they think of the whole report. So lead with the bottom line: in three or four sentences, what happened, how far it got, and the impact. Everything after is the evidence for that paragraph. Never make the reader assemble the story themselves.

Then a timeline of timed bullets (UTC), entry to objective, each line a real event with its timestamp. Then the findings, ordered by confidence. Then detections and gaps.

The six marking categories: write a section to each

Category What a top answer contains
Evidence Every claim has a row behind it. The exact query and its result, not a paraphrase.
Kill chain The attacker's steps in order: initial access → execution → persistence → privilege escalation → collection → exfil. Map each to a technique.
Scope Everything touched: every host, account, file, and connection. "What else did they do?" answered, not just the first flag.
Detection At least one authored detection rule (a KQL query that would catch this next time) and an honest false-positive discussion of what benign activity it would also flag.
Gaps What the telemetry could not show, and for each gap the specific change that closes it (enable command-line auditing, add a SACL, forward DNS).
Craft Clear prose, confidence labels, reproducible queries, consistent terminology.

Three lessons from a hunt debrief: bake these into every report

  1. A confidence label on every inference. Confirmed / Probable / Possible. "The account was created by the attacker (Confirmed: 4720 under the compromised session)" vs "likely used for persistence (Probable)." Guessing dressed as certainty is the fastest way to lose Evidence and Craft marks.
  2. Authored detections with their false positives. Don't just say "monitor for new admins." Give the query, then say plainly what legitimate activity it would also catch and how you'd tune it. A detection with no FP discussion reads as untested.
  3. Each gap paired with its fix. A gap is only useful if you say how to close it. "No command-line auditing, so we could not see script arguments → enable Audit Process Creation with command line, GPO X."

Findings, ordered by confidence

List findings strongest-evidence first. Each finding is: the claim, the confidence, the evidence (query + result), and what it means for the kill chain. One claim per finding; if a row supports two claims, make two findings.

Writing rules (house style)

  • Write the bottom-line summary once, and make every later retelling match it. Don't let the abstract and the conclusion disagree.
  • Plain language. State mechanisms directly; no filler, no drama.
  • One wording for anything you can't prove ("consistent with", "probable"), used consistently.
  • Name monitoring gaps explicitly; a gap you flag is a point you earn, a gap you hide is a point you lose.
  • No placement or scoring claims in a public writeup until the hunt's results are posted. (HuntCraft only publishes solved/closed hunts, so this is already satisfied, but keep the habit.)

Build the report from your own capture trail

Keep a record as you hunt: for each flag, the question, the exact query, the rows that answered it and what they told you. That record already is your Evidence and Kill-chain content. The report then adds the bottom line, the timeline, the authored detections with their false positives, and the gaps with their fixes. Because the evidence is the real data you queried, the report and your results can never disagree.
